One thousand. That is the light gap between a 99 percent eclipse and a 100 percent eclipse. At 99 percent the open sliver of photosphere still pours about 1,000 lux over you, the light of an overcast noon. Your pupils adapt and nothing feels wrong. At 100 percent the light drops to the level of a full moon. The corona unfolds, Venus switches on, and the horizon runs a 360 degree sunset. On Wednesday 12 August 2026 the total solar eclipse runs that experiment over nine countries in one evening.

Totality is not rare. Somewhere on Earth the moon covers the sun completely about every eighteen months. Rare means the shadow visits you. Peninsular Spain last stood under it in 1905. Iceland last did in 1954, and waits until 2196 for the next one.

Totality opens at midnight over Siberia with the sun scraping the horizon. It clips northeast Greenland, crosses western Iceland, and dies at dusk over the Balearics. Russia, Greenland, Iceland and Spain stand under it. Portugal, France, Denmark, the United Kingdom and Ireland get a deep partial. Every country reads its own map, so this paper covers the whole shadow. **A 99 percent eclipse is a 100 percent miss.**

## The moon fits the sun by accident

The sun is 400 times wider than the moon. It also sits 400 times farther away. Both disks span half a degree of sky, and one slides over the other like a coin over a coin.

The fit is tight, so the umbra barely reaches the ground. On 12 August it lands as an oval at most 294 kilometers wide. Astronomers call the strip it sweeps the path of totality. This paper calls it *the band*. Inside the oval, night in the middle of the day. One step outside, daylight with a dimmer. The gradient you expect does not exist. **The eclipse is binary.**

Timing shapes this edition. The umbra reaches Iberia at the end of the day, and a low sun drags the oval long across the map. Totality finds the sun 12 degrees high in A Coruña and 1.5 degrees high in Menorca. Those two numbers decide where you stand.

## A 99 percent eclipse is still daylight

The photosphere is absurdly bright. Cover 99 percent of it and the sliver still owns the sky.

- Clear noon: about 100,000 lux.
- 99 percent covered: about 1,000 lux. A dark overcast day. Streetlights stay off.
- Totality: moonlight. The full sun is 400,000 times brighter than the full moon.

The corona burns around the sun every day of the year, about as bright as the full moon. Daylight scatter buries it, and one percent of photosphere is still daylight. Only totality kills the glare. **The last percent is a light switch.**

At 99 percent you wear glasses for two hours and watch a bite leave an orange cookie. At 100 percent you drop the glasses and stand in a hole punched through the day. Two different events, half an hour of driving apart.

## The saros repeats this eclipse every 18 years

Three lunar clocks have to agree for an eclipse to repeat. Phase, node crossing, and distance. [NASA puts the agreement at 6,585.32 days](https://eclipse.gsfc.nasa.gov/SEsaros/SEsaros.html): 223 lunar months, 242 node returns, 239 distance returns, all landing within hours of each other. That interval is one *saros*. In plain units, 18 years, 11 days and 8 hours.

The 8 hours carry the payoff. Earth keeps turning through them, so the next eclipse of a series lands about a third of the way around the planet, westward. Watch it happen in one series. On 1 August 2008 the greatest eclipse fell at [65.7 north, 72.3 east](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_eclipse_of_August_1,_2008), near Nadym in Siberia. On 12 August 2026 it falls at 65.2 north, 25.2 west, off Iceland. Same latitude to within half a degree. Ninety seven degrees of longitude west.

This eclipse is number 48 of 72 in [Saros 126](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_Saros_126). The series was born on 10 March 1179 and dies on 3 May 2459. Number 41 fell on 28 May 1900 and crossed Spain, which is why [El Orden Mundial calls Wednesday a replica](https://elordenmundial.com/mapas-eclipse-espana/). Spain did not get lucky twice. Spain got the same eclipse back.

<Aside label='Saros // 126'>
- 72 eclipses over 1,280 years: 10 total, 28 annular, 3 hybrid, 31 partial.
- Even series number, so it runs at the descending node.
- Longest totality of the whole run: 2 minutes 36 seconds, on 10 July 1972.
- The band widens every cycle: 92 km in 1900, 237 in 2008, 294 now, 453 in 2044.
- About 40 series run at once. Every eclipse you have ever seen belongs to one.
</Aside>

## One shadow, nine countries

Russia opens the show at midnight, east of the Taymyr peninsula. The sun sits on the horizon line there, half in and half out, and the umbra catches it anyway. Cape Pronchishchev gets 1 minute 36 seconds. Almost nobody lives there, so the reindeer get the premiere.

The umbra then vaults the Arctic and clips northeast Greenland at 17:18 UTC. Station Nord, a Danish outpost, gets 20 seconds. That is the Kingdom of Denmark's entire slice of totality: a trailer, not a film. Copenhagen itself keeps 84 percent.

Iceland is the first stop with roads, beds and a full minute. The western half of the island sits inside the band, and the sun rides 25 degrees up. Reykjavík gets 1 minute 03 at 17:48 local. Keflavík airport, 50 kilometers down the road, gets 1 minute 41, so the arrivals hall beats the capital. The Látrabjarg cliffs at the western tip get 2 minutes 14, the longest totality on land anywhere on the band. The absolute maximum runs 2 minutes 18 seconds, 45 kilometers offshore. The best seat in the house is wet.

Spain gets the finale at dinner time. The umbra makes landfall at Estaca de Bares in Galicia at 20:26:37 and leaves Formentera at 20:33:58. Seven minutes and twenty one seconds for the whole country. It enters through the north coast of Galicia and Lugo. It sweeps the Cantabrian strip through Oviedo, Santander, Bilbao and Vitoria. It runs the Duero and the Ebro through León, Burgos, Valladolid, Logroño, Soria and Zaragoza. It exits by Teruel, Castelló, València, Tarragona and the Balearics. Everywhere the sun hangs low, because this is a sunset eclipse.

## Where to stand

Ninety minutes of driving for two minutes of sky. The trade sounds absurd. Then you price it. One thousand lux against moonlight. A partial eclipse against the corona. Drive.

1. Get deep into the band. Totality at the edge lasts seconds. The center line pays in full.
2. In Spain, audit the west-northwest horizon first. A 10 meter building 200 meters away blocks 3 degrees of sky. On the Mediterranean side that is the entire show. Find a shoreline, a ridge or a rooftop.
3. In Iceland the sun rides well clear of the horizon, so clouds decide everything. Keep a car and three escape roads.
4. Check the forecast at breakfast, commit by noon, and accept a 100 kilometer drive. A fixed plan is how people end up under the only cumulus in the province.

## Two rules for your eyes

The two rules are the two eclipses. Rule one governs the 99 percent world. Rule two exists only inside the last percent.

**Rule one: if any photosphere shows, filter.** Sunglasses fail. Smoked glass fails. Stacked film fails. Certified ISO 12312-2 glasses pass, and shade 14 welding glass passes. Cameras, binoculars and telescopes need filters on the front, because a filter behind the optics arrives too late. No filter at hand? A kitchen colander projects a dozen crescent suns on the ground for free. Portugal, France, the United Kingdom, Ireland and Denmark run rule one from first bite to last. There the glasses never come off.

**Rule two: during totality, look.** The glasses that protected you now show pure black. The corona is moonlight, and moonlight is safe. <u>During totality, and only during totality, watch with naked eyes.</u> Drop the glasses when the diamond ring collapses. Put them back at the first flash on the far edge.

<Aside label='Kit // Bring'>
- Eclipse glasses, certified to ISO 12312-2. One pair per head, and one adult per child.
- Shade 14 welding glass, the only common substitute.
- A kitchen colander, for crescent suns on the ground.
- A white sheet, for shadow bands.
- A full tank, water and layers. Roads into a 294 kilometer band do not scale.
</Aside>

## What the light does in the last hour

The hour before totality is already the event.

![Progression of the August 2017 total eclipse over Oregon, United States | Aubrey Gemignani (NASA)](./nasa-progression.webp)

Thirty minutes out the sun narrows to a slit, so shadows sharpen to knife edges and the light goes faintly green, then metallic. Cameras will not capture it. Around twenty minutes out the Purkinje shift arrives. Rod vision peaks near 507 nanometers and cone vision near 555, so [as the light dies your eyes hand over to the rods](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0042698902002675). Red collapses to gray. Green holds and turns luminous. [RTVE turned that into a national dress code](https://www.rtve.es/noticias/20260809/eclipse-solar-divertido-experimento-vestir-rojo-verde/17183151.shtml): wear red next to green and watch one of them quit.

Ten minutes out the temperature slides 2 or 3 degrees and the breeze turns. Birds fly home. At the 2015 Faroe Islands eclipse, [every dog in town howled at once](https://www.eldiario.es/sociedad/manual-disfrutar-eclipse-cinco-detalles-debes-fijarte-vivir-experiencia-inolvidable_1_13431722.html).

In the last minute, shadow bands ripple over white ground for 20 or 30 seconds, so bring a white sheet. Then face west-northwest and watch the umbra arrive as a wall of dusk. Baily's beads fire as sunlight threads the last lunar valleys. The final bead is the diamond ring.

Then the corona, white and structured, with pink prominences pinned to the rim. Venus first, then the brighter stars. Sunset orange on every horizon at once. People gasp and clap and cry, and the sound is part of the show. Your first eclipse deserves eyes. Instagram gets the second one.

## If the clouds win

Clouds delete the corona and keep the collapse. Under full overcast, totality still snaps day to night in seconds, the temperature still falls, the birds still panic. Stand in the band anyway.

Spain also holds a rematch. On 2 August 2027 a total eclipse crosses Cádiz, Málaga, Ceuta and Melilla, longer and higher in the sky. On 26 January 2028 an annular one runs southwest to northeast across the peninsula, Madrid included. Spain went 121 years with nothing, then booked [three solar eclipses in eighteen months](https://trioeclipses.es/). Iceland has no rematch until 2196, so chase the gaps there.

That night the Perseids peak, and an eclipse guarantees a new moon. The sky is dark by construction. Stay out.

Spain waited 121 years for this evening. Iceland waited 72. A 99 percent eclipse is a 100 percent miss. Get inside the band.
