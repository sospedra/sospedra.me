FTX filed Chapter 11 on November 11 and the commentary settled on its favorite conclusion: the gamblers were idiots. Millions of young people YOLOing savings into tokens, options and parlays, and the serious people shake their heads. Financial illiteracy, they say. Someone should teach these kids about compound interest.

Let's teach it, then, with the official numbers. Median gross salary in Spain: 20.920€ ([INE, Encuesta de Estructura Salarial](https://www.ine.es/prensa/ees_2020.pdf), and the *modal* salary, the one most people actually earn, sits far below that). Used housing in January 2023: 3.929€/m2 in Madrid, 4.050€/m2 in Barcelona ([idealista index](https://www.idealista.com/sala-de-prensa/informes-precio-vivienda/)). A modest 90m2 flat: ~355.000€. Save 20% of net income, a heroic rate on ~17.300€ net, and that's ~3.460€ a year against a 71.000€ deposit plus another ~10% in ITP and notary costs. **Twenty-three years to the deposit**, alone, assuming prices politely freeze while you save. They won't; they rose 5,4% last year. Two earners halve it to a mere decade of discipline for the *entry ticket*. The compound interest lesson teaches the opposite of what the serious people intended: the savings account is the one bet guaranteed to lose.

## The ladder that was

The postwar deal had a shape. Work, save, buy a home, let the home carry your retirement. Boring, deterministic, and it worked for roughly one generation, the one now writing op-eds about prudence. The Banco de España's own [household finance survey](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/servlet/articulo?codigo=8619773) draws the picture by cohort: each generation of Spaniards under 35 owns less home and less wealth at the same age than the one before. **The rungs didn't wear out. They were sold.**

A lottery ticket is irrational when the ladder works. When the ladder is gone, the math flips. *A deterministic path to nothing loses to a 2% chance at something.* The kids did not fail the math. **The kids did the math.** Crypto, meme stocks, 0DTE options, sports betting apps: four skins on the same instrument, variance as the only asset class the young can afford.

## The house always knew

None of this is new. The modern lottery is [Genoese](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/statistical-science/volume-6/issue-2/The-Genoese-Lottery/10.1214/ss/1177011819.full): citizens bet on which five of ninety candidates would be drawn for the city council, which is why your abuela's lotto is still a 5-of-90 game five centuries later. And Spain's own Lotería Nacional was born in a **war treasury emergency**: approved by the Cortes de Cádiz in November 1811, first draw in March 1812, a state under Napoleonic siege inventing a voluntary tax on hope. The pitch has never changed since: the state knows the ladder doesn't reach, so it sells tickets to the roof, and El Gordo becomes national liturgy.

The innovation of our decade is privatizing that franchise and adding a feed. Robinhood confetti. Exchange affiliates. Gambling logos on football shirts, a pitch so effective in Spain that the state had to kill it by decree: [Real Decreto 958/2020](https://www.boe.es/diario_boe/txt.php?id=BOE-A-2020-13495) banned bookmaker shirt sponsorship outright, after most of professional football had signed one. An attention economy wrapped around a variance economy, extracting from the same demographic the housing market already priced out. FTX was not a deviation from this system. It was the system, minus the actuarial discipline.

## Don't moralize the symptom

So no, I'm not interested in the financial literacy sermon. A generation bets because betting is the only instrument left with a visible path from their income to a dignified life. The scandal is not that they gamble. **The scandal is the base rate**: that a nurse's salary buys a nurse no home in the city she works in.

<u>Fix the ladder and the casino empties on its own.</u> Keep the ladder broken and every collapse, this one and the next, is just the house settling accounts. The gamblers were never the problem. The odds outside the casino were.
